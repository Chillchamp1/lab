Soundscapes for Headphones

Rendered binaurally for listening lying down: on your back, head on a pillow, gaze tilted upwards.

How to listen: headphones on (left and right the right way round), lie down, close your eyes. Every scene loops endlessly without an audible seam. Click a scene to open its own page – you can share that link.

Which ears suit you? The scenes are rendered with one set of measured ears (HRTF). The HRTF test finds out in 6–8 minutes which set matches your ears best. With in-ear microphones it gets more precise: ear measurement.

Do the spatial effects make a difference? In the blind comparison you hear the same passage with and without source width, directivity and tree-trunk echoes, without knowing which version is which.

How the scenes are made
  • Real recordings (public domain, CC0 or CC BY), cut into single events – calls, drops, footsteps, splashes – and placed anew in 3D space. Animals that no longer exist are made from real animal sounds, played slower and deeper. The only synthetic parts are the low layer that gives powerful moments their weight, and the music in the forest.
  • Head: HRTF CIPIC 040 (SOFA), diffuse-field equalised, smoothly interpolated between measured directions; no head tracking.
  • Space: every source arrives along several paths – direct, reflected off the ground, the hull or a tree trunk, diffracted over an edge – plus reverberation. Without space, everything sounds inside your head.
  • Movement: sources move along paths; the travel time is computed too, which produces the Doppler effect of a fly-by.
  • Endless loop: computed on a circular timeline; whatever rings on past the end continues at the start.
  • Levels: each sound group is set to its own target loudness (LUFS); rare peaks are caught by a look-ahead limiter.

Scripts, scene descriptions and the list of recordings are in the project folder (documentation in German).
